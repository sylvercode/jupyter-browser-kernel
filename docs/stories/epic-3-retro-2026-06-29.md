## What Went Well

1. Runtime ownership stayed explicit.

- Intentional capture remained helper-driven and extension-owned.
- Ambient browser console activity stayed out of notebook log rendering.

2. Output contract layering remained coherent.

- Value/error output and intentional logs coexisted without collapsing into one ambiguous blob.
- Failure paths preserved partial intentional logs, improving debuggability.

3. Test-backed delivery reduced risk.

- Story-level behavior changes were accompanied by unit and integration coverage updates.
- Reconnect/rerun behavior was validated for no backlog leakage.

4. Architectural boundaries held under feature growth.

- Kernel, transport, and notebook responsibilities remained separated.
- No raw protocol details leaked into user-facing output envelopes.

## Challenges and Friction

1. Tracking-state drift at epic level.

- Story-level statuses reached done while epic-level status remained in-progress.
- This weakens planning signal quality for follow-on epic readiness checks.

2. Requirement wording drift surfaced late.

- Distinction between notebook formatting semantics and output-channel filtering semantics required clarification during review.
- This was resolved, but the ambiguity consumed review energy that could have been avoided earlier.

3. Capability boundaries need continual reinforcement.

- Story 3.1 intentionally scoped helper availability for deterministic behavior.
- That boundary is correct, but future epics must keep this explicit to avoid accidental broadening.

## Key Insights

1. Intentional output succeeds when the emitter is explicit and scoped per run.
2. Separation of intentional and ambient output is both a UX and reliability concern, not just presentation polish.
3. Contract-level clarity in story acceptance wording prevents avoidable review churn.
4. Sprint tracking should enforce epic-state reconciliation when all epic stories and retrospective are complete.
