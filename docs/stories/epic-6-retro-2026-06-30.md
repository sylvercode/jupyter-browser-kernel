### What Went Well

1. Story 6.1 established deterministic fixtures that strengthened confidence in normalization, serialization boundaries, and debugger-source identity.
2. Story 6.2 proved forward and rollback workflows in one live session without reconnect overhead or hidden state resets.
3. Story 6.3 used traceability and existing test evidence to avoid duplicate implementation work.
4. Epic 6 preserved the shared result contract and profile-agnostic core boundaries throughout all three stories.
5. Validation posture remained strong: compile and CDP integration checks were used as release-shaping evidence instead of relying on intuition.

### Challenges and Friction

1. Epic-level sprint tracking drifted behind story reality; all Epic 6 stories were done while the epic remained marked in-progress.
2. Story 6.3 wording still carried history-adjacent language that could have pulled implementation toward an out-of-scope execution-history feature.
3. Review corrections in Stories 6.1 and 6.2 show that assertion quality can still be too permissive unless contract details are checked explicitly.
4. The team relied on story-local evidence to understand reliability progress; there is still no standardized epic-closeout checklist tying those signals together.

### Key Lessons Learned

1. Reliability stories need explicit proof obligations. Fixture identity, contract keys, and classification details should be treated as first-class acceptance evidence.
2. When a story overlaps completed capability, AC-to-test traceability should happen before any implementation starts.
3. Safe experimentation belongs in existing notebook and execution contracts, not in a new history or workflow subsystem.
4. Epic closeout needs a final reconciliation pass so epic-level status, retrospective status, and story-level truth stay aligned.
5. Review findings that catch permissive assertions are process signals, not just test fixes; they show where guardrails need tightening earlier.
